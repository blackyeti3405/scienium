# Science Research Plan — Review Rubric (base)

> Loaded for every science project, then specialized by a subtype file. Based on
> the Regeneron ISEF **Science** judging criteria (docs/isef-2026.md).

You are reviewing an early **research plan** for a high-school science project —
not finished results. Judge whether the *plan* sets the team up to score well at
ISEF, and give concrete, actionable guidance. Calibrate to a strong advanced/AP
high-school researcher: demand rigor, not graduate-level jargon.

Grade the plan against these five sections (ISEF Science point weights shown):

- **I. Research Question (10):** Is there a clear, focused, **testable** question
  or hypothesis? Does the plan explain its contribution to the field — why the
  answer matters and what is currently unknown?
- **II. Design & Methodology (15):** Is the experimental plan well-designed?
  Are the **independent, dependent, and controlled variables** identified? Are
  controls and sample sizes appropriate, and the data-collection method complete?
- **III. Execution: Data, Analysis & Interpretation (20):** Will the plan yield
  enough data to support a conclusion? Are the planned **analysis / statistical
  methods** appropriate? Is the work **reproducible** as described?
- **IV. Creativity & Potential Impact (20):** Is the question original or the
  approach inventive? Does the plan articulate real or potential impact (field,
  technology, environment, society)?
- **V. Presentation readiness (35):** Will the plan produce the evidence and
  analysis needed for a strong poster + interview? Does the team show they
  understand the underlying science and the limitations of their design?

Reward a sharp testable hypothesis, well-defined variables and controls, and a
realistic analysis plan. Penalize a vague/untestable question, missing controls,
confounded variables, and "collect data and see what happens."

# Brief — classical-mechanics-physics · rotational-mechanics (branch)

- **branch id / label:** `rotational-mechanics` · **Rotational Dynamics & Rigid Bodies**
- **target:** 8 units × ~6 lessons ≈ **48 lessons**
- **idPrefix:** `cmp-rotational` (lesson ids → `cmp-rotational-<unitShort>-<slug>`)
- **scope:** Torque, moment of inertia, angular momentum and its conservation, rolling vs sliding, and rotational kinetic energy — the angular analogs of the Core's linear mechanics.
- **boundaries:** Core is deliberately linear; ALL rotation lives here. Orbital angular momentum in a gravity field belongs to `gravitation-orbits`; keep this to rigid-body rotation about an axis (spin, rolling, precession).

## Unit plan (angular kinematics → torque/statics → I → τ=Iα → energy/rolling → L → landmark → applications)
1. **Angular Kinematics** — angular position/velocity/acceleration; radians; the linear↔angular links v = rω and a_tangential = rα (plus centripetal a = rω²); the rotational analog of the constant-acceleration equations. *(6)*
2. **Torque and Rotational Equilibrium** — torque τ = rF·sinθ and the lever arm; net torque; static equilibrium (ΣF = 0 AND Στ = 0); center of gravity; solving ladder, beam, and see-saw problems. *(6)*
3. **Moment of Inertia** — rotational inertia I = Σmr²; I for a hoop, disk, rod, and sphere; the parallel-axis theorem; why *where* the mass sits (not just how much) governs rotation. *(6)*
4. **Newton's Second Law for Rotation (τ = Iα)** — the angular analog of F = ma; massive pulleys, unwinding spools, and yo-yos; combining translation and rotation in one problem. *(6)*
5. **Rotational Energy and Rolling Motion** — rotational KE = ½Iω²; rolling without slipping (v = rω, and the role of static friction); the classic ramp race (hoop vs disk vs sphere decided by I); rolling vs sliding energy. *(6)*
6. **Angular Momentum and Its Conservation** — L = Iω (and L = r×p); conservation when net torque is zero; the figure-skater and springboard diver; gyroscopes and precession (qualitative). *(7)*
7. **Landmark Work & Open Debates** *(required)* — Euler's rigid-body equations and the spinning top; the intermediate-axis (tennis-racket / Dzhanibekov) theorem and its surprising instability; the gyrocompass and gyroscopic navigation; the falling-cat angular-momentum puzzle (zero-L reorientation); Newton's rotating-bucket and the absolute-vs-relative-rotation debate (Mach's critique). *(6)*
8. **Applications: Gyroscopes, Flywheels, and Spinning Sports** — flywheel energy storage; gyroscopes for phones and spacecraft attitude control; angular momentum in gymnastics, diving, and discus; wheels and gears; designing a defensible rotational experiment (extracting I from measured angular acceleration). *(5)*

**Total: 6+6+6+6+6+7+6+5 = 48 lessons.**

## Depth cues
Named work: Euler (rigid-body dynamics), Poinsot (rotation geometry), the Dzhanibekov effect (1985), the falling-cat studies (Marey's 1894 chronophotography), Newton's bucket, Mach's principle. Debates: intermediate-axis instability; is rotation absolute (Newton) or relative (Mach)? Applications: reaction/momentum wheels in satellites, gyroscopic phone/vehicle sensors, flywheel storage, sports spin control. Pairs-with the Core (as the angular analog) and `collisions-momentum`.

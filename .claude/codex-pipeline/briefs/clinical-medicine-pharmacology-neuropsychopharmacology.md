## Scope (TAXONOMY)
Drugs acting on the brain: neurotransmitter systems, receptor agonism/antagonism, analgesics,
caffeine/stimulants and reaction-time studies, and psychoactive-drug mechanisms. Angle is the
DRUG and its dose-response, NOT the neural circuitry itself (that's the neuroscience field).

## Unit plan — 7 units, ~45 lessons
1. `clinpharm-neuro-foundations` **Neuropharmacology Foundations** (6) — the synapse and chemical neurotransmission; ionotropic (fast, ligand-gated) vs metabotropic (slow, GPCR) receptors; reuptake and enzymatic termination; the blood-brain barrier as a drug filter; the mechanistic vocabulary (agonist / antagonist / partial agonist / reuptake inhibitor / enzyme inhibitor); CNS dose-response.
2. `clinpharm-neuro-transmitters` **The Major Neurotransmitter Systems** (7) — dopamine, serotonin, norepinephrine, GABA (inhibitory), glutamate (excitatory), acetylcholine, and the endorphin/opioid system — the function of each and the drug classes that target it. A matching-heavy unit (transmitter → drug → effect).
3. `clinpharm-neuro-stimulants` **Stimulants, Caffeine & Reaction Time** (6) — caffeine as an adenosine-receptor antagonist; nicotine; amphetamine/methylphenidate (dopamine/NE); the dose-response on alertness and reaction time; the inverted-U (Yerkes-Dodson) arousal-performance idea; the canonical student caffeine-reaction-time study setup.
4. `clinpharm-neuro-analgesics` **Analgesics & Pain Pharmacology** (6) — the pain pathway (nociception → transmission → perception); opioids (mu-receptor agonism, dose-response, tolerance, respiratory depression); NSAIDs (COX inhibition); acetaminophen; local anesthetics (sodium-channel block). Mechanism → measurable analgesia and its ceiling.
5. `clinpharm-neuro-psychoactive` **Psychoactive & Psychiatric Drugs** (7) — antidepressants (SSRIs/SNRIs, the reuptake mechanism and the delayed onset); anxiolytics/sedatives (benzodiazepines and GABA-A); antipsychotics (dopamine D2 blockade, typical vs atypical); mood stabilizers (lithium); alcohol's multi-target CNS depression. Mechanism → behavioral effect.
6. `clinpharm-neuro-landmark` **Landmark Work & Open Debates** (7) **[landmark]** — Otto Loewi's frog-heart experiment proving chemical neurotransmission (1921; 1936 Nobel with Dale); the discovery of chlorpromazine (1952, Laborit/Delay/Deniker) and the psychopharmacology revolution; the serotonin "chemical-imbalance" theory of depression and its collapse; the SSRI efficacy / publication-bias debate (Kirsch meta-analyses); the opioid crisis and the history of the pain-as-fifth-vital-sign push; tolerance and dependence mechanisms. VERIFY names/dates with WebSearch.
7. `clinpharm-neuro-apply` **Applications & Safe Study Design** (6) — safe study designs using ONLY caffeine or non-drug manipulations; placebo, blinding, and expectancy effects (huge in CNS studies); confounders (practice/habituation effect, sleep, prior caffeine); measuring reaction time, the Stroop task, and simple cognition; ethics and the bright line (students never dose psychoactive/prescription drugs); a fully worked caffeine-and-reaction-time mini-project.

## Depth requirements
Landmark people/dates (Loewi 1921/1936 Nobel; chlorpromazine 1952); debates (serotonin-imbalance
theory, SSRI publication bias, opioid over-prescription); competing methods (typical vs atypical
antipsychotics, placebo-controlled vs open designs). Judge framing specific per lesson.

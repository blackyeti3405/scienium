# Brief — classical-mechanics-physics · oscillations-shm (branch)

- **branch id / label:** `oscillations-shm` · **Oscillations & Simple Harmonic Motion**
- **target:** 7 units × ~6 lessons ≈ **44 lessons**
- **idPrefix:** `cmp-oscillations` (lesson ids → `cmp-oscillations-<unitShort>-<slug>`)
- **scope:** Springs and pendulums as SHM — restoring force, period/frequency laws, damping, driven oscillation and mechanical resonance, and phase-space analysis.
- **boundaries:** Mechanical vibration ONLY. The `acoustics-sound` field owns sound and pressure waves; do not drift into wave propagation, standing waves in air, or audio. Stay on the oscillator itself (mass-spring, pendulum, damped/driven single-DOF systems).

## Unit plan (anatomy → spring → pendulum → math → damping → resonance → landmark)
1. **The Anatomy of an Oscillation** — restoring force toward equilibrium; amplitude, period, frequency, angular frequency ω; what makes motion *simple* harmonic (linear restoring force F = −kx); periodic vs SHM. *(6)*
2. **The Mass–Spring Oscillator** — Hooke's law; T = 2π√(m/k); isochronism (period independent of amplitude for ideal SHM); energy exchange KE↔PE; velocity and acceleration vs displacement. *(6)*
3. **The Pendulum** — simple pendulum T = 2π√(L/g); the small-angle approximation sinθ ≈ θ and where it breaks; the physical (compound) pendulum; using a pendulum to measure g by linearization. *(7)*
4. **The Mathematics of SHM** — x(t) = A·cos(ωt + φ); phase and phase constant; velocity/acceleration as derivatives; the reference-circle picture; the phase portrait (v vs x) as an ellipse. *(6)*
5. **Damped Oscillations** — a velocity-proportional damping force; underdamped, critically damped, and overdamped regimes; the exponential decay envelope and time constant; the quality factor Q; log-decrement measurement. *(6)*
6. **Driven Oscillations, Resonance, and Applications** — forced oscillation; resonance at the natural frequency; amplitude and phase response vs drive frequency; Q and bandwidth; engineering uses (tuned mass dampers, car suspension, seismometers, MEMS resonators) and the Tacoma Narrows nuance (aeroelastic flutter, not textbook resonance). *(7)*
7. **Landmark Work & Open Debates** *(required)* — Galileo and pendulum isochronism (the cathedral-chandelier story); Huygens' pendulum clock (1656) and the cycloidal correction for large swings; Hooke's law (1678, the *ceiiinosssttuv* anagram); the Tacoma Narrows resonance-vs-flutter debate; anharmonicity — the real pendulum's amplitude-dependent period. *(6)*

**Total: 6+6+7+6+6+7+6 = 44 lessons.**

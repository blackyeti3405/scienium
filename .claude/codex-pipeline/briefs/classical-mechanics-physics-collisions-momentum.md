# Brief — classical-mechanics-physics · collisions-momentum (branch)

- **branch id / label:** `collisions-momentum` · **Collisions, Impulse & Momentum**
- **target:** 7 units × ~6 lessons ≈ **44 lessons**
- **idPrefix:** `cmp-collisions` (lesson ids → `cmp-collisions-<unitShort>-<slug>`)
- **scope:** Conservation of momentum in 1D and 2D — elastic vs inelastic collisions, coefficient of restitution, impulse from force–time curves, and center-of-mass motion of interacting bodies.
- **boundaries:** Core introduced momentum, impulse, and 1D elastic/inelastic at a foundational level; this branch goes DEEP — real force–time curves, the restitution coefficient, 2D vector collisions, the zero-momentum frame. Rotational angular momentum belongs to `rotational-mechanics`; keep this to linear/translational momentum.

## Unit plan (impulse depth → 1D conservation → elastic → restitution → 2D → CM frame → landmark)
1. **Momentum and Impulse in Depth** — J = ∫F dt as the area under a real force–time curve; average vs peak force; measuring an impulse with a force plate/sensor; the impulse behind a good vs a jarring landing. *(6)*
2. **Conservation of Momentum in One Dimension** — closed systems and the vanishing of internal forces (3rd law); recoil and explosions; a systematic sign convention for solving 1D collision problems. *(6)*
3. **Elastic Collisions** — momentum AND kinetic energy conserved; the 1D elastic result (equal masses exchange velocities; relative velocity reverses); limiting cases (into a wall, into a much heavier/lighter body); Newton's cradle done right. *(6)*
4. **Inelastic Collisions and the Coefficient of Restitution** — perfectly inelastic (objects stick, max KE lost); e = |v_separation|/|v_approach|; measuring e from bounce height (e = √(h′/h)); the ballistic pendulum; crash-safety energy absorption. *(7)*
5. **Two-Dimensional Collisions** — momentum conservation component-by-component; glancing collisions; the 90° separation of equal-mass elastic 2D collisions (billiards); explosions and fragmentation in 2D. *(6)*
6. **Center of Mass and the Zero-Momentum Frame** — the CM velocity is constant in a closed system; working a collision in the CM (zero-momentum) frame; reduced mass; why the CM frame simplifies the algebra. *(6)*
7. **Landmark Work & Open Debates** *(required)* — the 1668 Royal Society collision problem answered by Wallis, Wren, and Huygens (the birth of momentum conservation and elastic-collision theory); what Newton's cradle really does and does not demonstrate; crash-test dummies and impulse-based safety engineering; the "is restitution a material constant?" debate (e depends on speed, temperature, geometry). *(7)*

**Total: 6+6+6+7+6+6+7 = 44 lessons.**

## Depth cues
Named work: Wallis/Wren/Huygens (1668–69 Royal Society), Huygens' elastic-collision rules, Robins' & the ballistic pendulum, Gadd/crash-injury criteria. Debates: restitution's speed/material dependence; Newton's-cradle misconceptions; energy-vs-momentum bookkeeping in a "sticky" collision. Applications: automotive crumple zones/airbags (impulse), sport COR regulation (baseball bat "trampoline effect," golf, tennis), rocket/jet propulsion, particle-collision reasoning (conceptual). Pairs-with the Core momentum unit and `rotational-mechanics` (angular-momentum analog).

# Review: Hydrology Bay (terrabase) — round 0

**VERDICT: TWEAK**

The masonry hall, header tank, gauge mast, and `o`/`K` striped apron are all present and the palette is clean and in-spec — but the two load-bearing elements of this blueprint are not landing: (a) the **arched channel mouth** that defines the BUILT FORM is missing, so the hall reads as a generic hipped-roof shed/cube, and (b) the signature **flume** reads as scattered teal puddles rather than a terraced channel of flowing water with a legible dye-trace fault. Both are automatic-TWEAK conditions (criteria 6 and 8).

## Per-criterion

1. **Iso 2:1?** Yes. Roof ridge and wall edges hold a clean 2:1 isometric slope. Header tank cylinder reads correctly in iso. PASS.
2. **Roof/floor visible (3/4 top-down)?** Yes — the low hipped `L`/`M` roof is fully visible from above, and the courtyard apron is visible at ground level in front. PASS.
3. **Front corner faces viewer?** Yes, the front vertical corner of the hall points at the camera with the left (`D`-darker) and right faces splaying off it. PASS.
4. **Sharp/clean/in-palette/clean K outline?** Mostly PASS on cleanliness — 15 colors, all map exactly to the set palette (`K W L M D d h c g G o O r`), no anti-alias, crisp single-pixel edges, hard `K` outline. BUT the busy lower-left is the painter-flagged weak spot and it IS muddy: the dye cloud + fault + 2nd gauge collapse into a single teal-and-amber blob with no internal `K` separation between trough lip, dye plume, and gauge post (see lower-left crop). The `K` outline is clean as a silhouette but absent *within* that cluster.
5. **Cohesive TerraBase palette + motifs?** Yes — earthy `M`/`D` masonry, `L` canvas-toned roof, mineral-teal `c`/`g` glow, warm amber `o`/`O` accents, `o`/`K` survey striping on the apron edge. Reads as TerraBase. PASS.
6. **One-of-one signature present + legible + distinct?** FAIL. The teal in the courtyard is a set of disconnected `c`/`g` blobs. There is no clear **terraced channel**: no 3–4 stacked `D`/`M` troughs with `K` lips stepping down front-left, no `W` riffle highlights marking each fall, so it does not read as *flowing terraced water*. The **dye-trace fault** does not read — there is an amber cross/glow in the lower-left blob but no legible "dye plume bunching at a `r`/`d` crack" story; it looks like a generic glow, not a diagnosed leak. The **two flow gauges** are not legible as dials with needles — gauge B's red low-flow needle (the painter's flagged subtle element) is not findable at all. Net: the hero does not communicate "watershed flume + dye-trace fault diagnosis." It also risks reading closer to a generic teal-water splash than a terraced channel.
7. **Attractive?** Partially. The upper two-thirds (hall, roof, header tank, supply pipe arc, gauge mast with `c` blink) are genuinely attractive and well-built. The lower courtyard drags it down — it looks cluttered and unresolved rather than purposeful.
8. **Unique built form + opening OPEN?** FAIL on built form. The stated hook — a solid masonry hall whose FRONT face is cut by a large `K`-outlined **arched channel mouth** with a dark `d`/`K` interior and water emerging — is not present. The front-right face is a flat planked `M`/`D` wall with an amber valve box; there is no arch, no hollow, no channel emerging from the hall. As built, this is a closed hipped-roof shed with teal piled in front of it = a generic box, which is an automatic TWEAK. The OPEN courtyard apron exists, but without the arched mouth the "open-fronted water-yard *hall*" concept doesn't read, and it's not yet clearly distinct from the other OPEN sibling (Sampling's tarp depot).

## Tweak list (numbered, painter-actionable)

1. **Cut the arched channel mouth into the front-right face (the hook).** On the front-right wall, roughly the lower-center span beneath the roof eave (the area currently flat `M`/`D` planks above the valve box), carve a large arch: `K`-outline a rounded-top opening ~6–8px wide and ~7–9px tall, fill the interior dark `d` shading to `K` at the back. This is the single most important change — without it the form is a generic cube.
2. **Send the channel water out of the arch.** From the base of the new arch, run a short `c`/`g` water tongue (with one `W` riffle pixel) spilling down onto the courtyard so the flume visibly *originates from the hall mouth*, tying the signature to the form.
3. **Rebuild the flume as a legible terraced channel.** Replace the scattered teal blobs in the courtyard with 3–4 stacked troughs descending front-left: each trough is a `D`/`M` box with a bright `K` lip on its downhill edge, `c`/`g` water inside, and a row of `W` riffle highlights at each step-down fall. The eye should read distinct *steps*, not a puddle.
4. **Make the dye-trace fault read as a diagnosis.** At ONE trough (mid-flume), inject a brighter `G`/`g` teal dye plume that visibly *bunches/stalls* — wider and denser than the flow above/below it. At that same trough put the fault: a short `r`/`d` crack (2–3px) and a small `O`/`r` glow halo so it reads "the leak the dye revealed," not a random highlight. Currently the amber cross in the lower-left reads as decoration.
5. **Make the two flow gauges legible.** Give each gauge a clear `K`-framed dial (a 3×3 or 4×4 `K` box with `W`/`L` face) on a `D` stub post. Gauge A near the top reads normal (`o`/`O` needle mid-swing); Gauge B at the fault swings to a red `r` low-flow needle — make that needle a clear 2–3px `r` stroke, not a single subtle pixel, since it's the diagnosis payoff.
6. **De-clutter the lower-left.** Separate the dye cloud, fault glow, and gauge-B cluster with `K` outline pixels and a little dark `d` negative space so the three elements don't merge into one teal-amber mass. Drop any stray teal pixels that aren't part of a trough.
7. **(Minor) Add the `o` "FLOW" placard at the flume head** if room allows after the above — a tiny 3–4px `o` tag near the top trough — to label the rig and reinforce the survey-yard read.

## Optional polish (after the above)
- The header tank, supply-pipe arc, and gauge-mast `c` blink are strong; keep them. Once the arch + flume land, consider a faint `c` water-glow wash over the courtyard floor (a few `c` pixels on the `L` apron) to unify the yard.
